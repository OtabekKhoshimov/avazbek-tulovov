Where did my love of books begin?

«Children usually follow not our advice, but the way we live.»

I spent my childhood in an ordinary village in the Pakhtachi district.
My father was a farmer.
And in our house there was a bookcase full of books.
Sometimes my father would say, “Don’t touch the books.”
Thinking about it now, perhaps that is exactly how he made me even more curious about them.

One day I noticed a book on the windowsill: Khudoyberdi Tukhtaboyev’s “Riding the Yellow Giant”.
I could not even read fluently yet. Syllable by syllable, I read it to the end.
After that, books became an inseparable part of my life.
On the way to school, my bag always held two or three books besides the textbooks.
Even when I went out to graze the cattle, I took a book with me.

By the eighth grade I had started reading the works of our Jadid forefathers.
Their thoughts on the future of the nation, on knowledge and enlightenment, had a powerful effect on me.

Looking back today, I understand one thing even better.
One of the most important environments shaping a child’s future is their home.
We want our children to read books.
But are books valued in our home?
We want our children to be educated.
But how much time do we ourselves devote to learning?
Children usually follow not our advice, but the way we live.

So I have a question for parents:
Which of your habits is your child inheriting from you?
