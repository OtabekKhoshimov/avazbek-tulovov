Why are people afraid of change?

«The hardest change in an organisation is not replacing the technology, but changing people’s attitude to their work.»

I remember when we began digitising management processes at Registon.
We decided to move student records, attendance, payments and other processes into a single system.
Our goal was simple: bring order, reduce errors and improve management.

But we ran into an unexpected problem.
Some of the staff resisted the new system.
For years they had been used to paper registers, handwritten records and Excel spreadsheets.
Entering data into the new system felt like extra work to them.

That was when I understood something.
The hardest change in an organisation is not replacing the technology, but changing people’s attitude to their work.

Today, as we bring artificial intelligence into the management of our company, I remember that experience.
New technology does not produce results by itself.
The people who use it have to understand why it matters and accept a new way of working.
A leader’s job is not simply to buy new software.
A leader must be able to explain to the team why the change is needed.

There is no guarantee that a method which worked well yesterday will still be the best one tomorrow.

What do you think holds an organisation back more: a lack of opportunities, or the inability to let go of old habits?
