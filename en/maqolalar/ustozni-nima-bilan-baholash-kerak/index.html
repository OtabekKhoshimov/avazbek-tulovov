How should we measure a teacher?

«The true value of education lies not in the number of lessons taught, but in the change that happens in the student.»

2013.

I suggested to my student Alisher that he teach history to university applicants.
He hesitated.
“But I haven’t even got into university myself,” he said.
I told him I believed in his knowledge, and that what mattered most was that he could get results for his students.

When I remember that conversation today, a question comes to mind.
What do we judge teachers by?
Their diploma? Their years of service? Their certificates?
Of course, all of these matter. A teacher’s qualifications and professional training cannot be dismissed.

But for me the most important question is a different one:
Is the child who learned from this teacher more knowledgeable today than yesterday?
Has the student’s thinking grown? Has their self-confidence increased? Have new opportunities opened up for their future?

Sometimes we get so absorbed in the process of teaching that we forget its real purpose.
Parents do not send their child to school simply to sit through lessons.
They expect results.
The true value of education lies not in the number of lessons taught, but in the change that happens in the student.

I believe every teacher should keep asking themselves one question:
What changed in my student after today’s lesson?
