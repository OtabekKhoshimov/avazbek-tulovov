# What matters most in a school?
> To treat every child as if they were our own.

When we went to Turkey to study how their schools work, one moment caught my attention.
We entered a classroom in one of the schools in Istanbul.
An ordinary room.
No luxurious equipment, no cutting-edge technology in sight.
Desks neatly arranged. On the walls, work made by the children themselves.

But the room had a different atmosphere.
Everything was arranged with such care and attention that a thought crossed my mind:
**“Children are loved here.”**
That impression later played an important part in shaping the educational concept of Registon schools.

We often judge a school’s quality by its building, its equipment, its laboratories.
Of course, these matter.
**But isn’t how a child feels when they come to school every day even more important?**
Does the teacher listen to them?
When they make a mistake, are they helped to understand, or humiliated?
Do they feel safe among their classmates?
Does the school see their abilities?

In an environment where a child does not feel valued, even the most modern technology may not deliver the expected results.
That is why in our schools we pay special attention to one principle:
**To treat every child as if they were our own.**

In my view, a school’s true quality begins with exactly this attitude.
