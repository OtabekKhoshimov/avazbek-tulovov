# From Bukhara, looking at the world...
> I want the world to come to this land for knowledge again, as it once did.

Bukhara.
A city that for centuries was one of the great centres of knowledge and enlightenment.
Great scholars lived here, and thousands of students studied in its madrasas.
The Mir Arab, Ulugbek and Abdulazizkhan madrasas...
These are not merely historical monuments.
**They are vivid proof of the attention once given to knowledge, and of our people’s intellectual potential.**

Today I am looking out over the city from the top floor of the Registon school in Bukhara.
All kinds of thoughts pass through my mind.
Fitrat once lived in this city.
He and the other Jadid thinkers tied the future of the nation to education, science and enlightenment.
They fought to open new schools and to teach young people modern knowledge.

More than a century has passed.
But in my view their most important question is still relevant:
**How do we raise our nation through knowledge?**

Today we too have opened a school in Bukhara.
That is, of course, a joy.
But for me the greatest achievement is not a beautiful building.
**It is the kind of people who will come out of this school.**
Perhaps one of the children sitting in our classroom today will become a great scientist.
Another a world-renowned engineer, doctor or entrepreneur.
Another will make a great contribution to the future of our nation.
We must create opportunities for every one of them.

A few years may be enough to build a school.
But raising a strong generation takes decades of consistent work.
That is our goal.
**I want the world to come to this land for knowledge again, as it once did.**

**What must we do today for this dream to come true?**
